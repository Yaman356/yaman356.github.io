## 0. Baseline and per-game mechanical breakdown (core loop, supply chain, session structure)

### Takeaway
The references split into three families: (a) real player-economy sims (Sim Companies, Virtonomics, Prosperous Universe, EVE, Albion) where the supply chain and the market between players are the game; (b) casual production-chain games (Hay Day, Township) with NPC order boards and tightly capped player trade; (c) single-player or offline-style wealth fantasies (Business Empire: RichMan, Idle Miner Tycoon, Big Ambitions, Capitalism Lab). Ticarium sits between (a) and (c): it markets itself as an online multiplayer trade sim but its developer deliberately blocks player-to-player money transfers.

### Inferences
- Ticarium's closest structural peer is Sim Companies (production plus retail plus player exchange), while its store-review complaints (no tutorial, punitive taxes and licence fees, facility cap) are the same complaint classes seen for Virtonomics (onboarding, obscure mechanics, paywalled growth).
- Business Empire: RichMan reaching about 19M installs with shallow, mostly offline mechanics, versus Sim Companies' small Steam concurrency, suggests the mass market rewards wealth fantasy and low friction more than simulation depth; depth serves retention of a smaller core.
- Local Turkish peers (Ticaret Simülatör) compete on breadth of asset types (cars, hotels, trucks) and on status ranks rather than on market simulation.

## 1. How does each game set prices: fixed NPC prices, simulated demand curves, or a real order book between players? What are the trade-offs?

### Takeaway
The strongest references combine two layers: a player-to-player market for intermediate goods (order book or lowest-ask listing) and a simulated consumer demand sink for end products that responds to aggregate supply. Pure fixed or capped pricing (Hay Day) is safe but widely criticised as a non-market; pure order books (Prosperous Universe, EVE) are praised by the core but suffer from thin liquidity and fragment easily.

### Cited Findings
- Sim Companies Exchange is a sell-listing market sorted by lowest price, with higher quality ranked first at equal price and a minimum-quality filter; sales carry a 3% fee — [Sim Companies beginner guide](https://simcompanies.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GUIDES/pages/196609/Guide+for+beginners)
- Sim Companies contracts (direct player-to-player deals) avoid the 3% fee and need half the transport units — [Sim Companies FAQ](https://simcompanies.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GUIDES/pages/1540097/Frequently+asked+questions)
- Sim Companies retail is a simulated demand model: once a day the model counts goods sold at retail across the whole economy and sets next-day demand per product from saturation; demand dictates sale speed; when demand is low, higher quality is needed — [Sim Companies Economy Model](https://guides.simcompanies.com/books/economy-model/export/markdown)
- Sim Companies macro cycle: three phases (Recession, Normal, Boom); each Friday the phase may change, moving one step or staying; recession raises production output and lowers retail sale volume, boom the reverse — [Sim Companies Economy Model](https://simcompanies.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GUIDES/pages/7536669/Economy+model)
- Sim Companies supporters get per-quality price charts with candlesticks and VWAP (recent Steam patch note) — [Steam community hub](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2253720)
- Prosperous Universe uses true order books run by exchange brokers — [PrUn devlog #358](https://prosperousuniverse.com/blog/2022/10/24/broken-brokers-358)
- Prosperous Universe liquidity problem: a developer noted that local-market ads drain liquidity from thin exchange brokers, which hurts free players most since they cannot access local markets — [PrUn devlog #348](https://prosperousuniverse.com/blog/2022/08/15/to-sell-or-not-to-sell-348); community thread on liquidity — [PrUn forum](https://com.prosperousuniverse.com/t/improving-liquidity-is-key-to-an-economy/4544?page=2)
- EVE Online: regional order books; base sales tax 7.5% reduced by skills; broker fee 3% base in NPC stations charged at order creation and not refunded on cancel or expiry; changing an order's price adds a relist charge; in player structures the broker fee is split between the owner and an NPC sink — [EVE University: Sales tax](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Sales_tax); [EVE University: Trading](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Trading); [CCP: Broker Relations](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/broker-relations)
- EVE fee figures at max skill conflict between wiki pages (4.6% vs 3.37%) — [EVE University: Station Trading](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Station_Trading); [EVE University: Sales tax](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Sales_tax)
- Albion: separate per-city order markets; price gaps between cities are the reward for hauling — [GuildOrder wiki](https://guildorder.com/games/albion/wiki/market-arbitrage-and-regional-pricing)
- Virtonomics: retail prices are set by the player against a simulated city market; a forum thread says many players recommend keeping at most about 15% of market volume for long-term operation (player heuristic) — [Virtonomics forum](https://virtonomics.com/mary/forum/forum_new/13/topic/105136/view)
- Virtonomics criticism: daily manual price and supply re-tuning with no automation; obscure behaviour such as stores ignoring an underserved city market — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/virtonomics.com)
- Hay Day roadside shop: prices are capped (players complain everyone lists at the maximum and propose an "Overpriced" stamp) — [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/hay-day/id506627515)
- Economist Vili Lehdonvirta called Hay Day's roadside shop "how not to do it" for player-to-player exchange, citing the workaround of posting a cheap placeholder item to signal availability and then swapping in the real product; he argues player economies are hard to maintain but raise engagement (older) — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/player-to-player-economies-why-mobile-needs-to-do-it-and-how-hay-day-did-it-badly)
- Big Ambitions is criticised for static costs and no dynamic competition; a player wanted responsive demand and rivals that undercut, citing Capitalism Lab as the higher bar — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1331550/discussions/0/3790381090376297959/)
- AirlineSim demand is defined per airport, with passenger classes as a fixed percentage split (2019, older); the team later planned booking classes and fare codes — [AirlineSim blog](https://airlinesim.aero/blog/2019/09/21/demand-in-airlinesim)

### Inferences
- The Sim Companies hybrid (player exchange for intermediates plus daily-recomputed saturation for retail plus a weekly macro phase) is the most transferable pricing design for a mobile-first title: prices move from player behaviour without needing deep order-book liquidity.
- Transaction fees do two jobs at once: money sink and anti-flipping friction. EVE's non-refundable broker fee plus relist charge specifically targets order-undercutting spam; Sim Companies' fee-free contracts push players into relationships. Both are reusable levers.
- A single global lowest-ask list (Sim Companies) concentrates liquidity; splitting into local markets (Albion, PrUn local markets) adds geography gameplay but thins each book. For a player base the size of Ticarium's, one global market per good with a quality filter is the safer default.
- Hard price caps avoid exploitation and real-money trading but remove price discovery and invite signalling workarounds; a price band (floor and ceiling around a moving reference) would be a middle path. This is inference; no source tested it.

### Gaps
- Exact Sim Companies retail formula and phase-change probabilities were not retrievable.
- Virtonomics' retail demand formula (city wealth, brand, quality, price elasticity) was not found in English sources.
- No source on how Township's player market/dealer sets prices.
- RichMan and Ticaret Simülatör pricing models (fixed vs simulated) are not documented in sources found.

## 2. How does each handle quality/R&D, logistics/transport costs, and location/geography?

### Takeaway
Quality is most cleanly handled in Sim Companies (discrete quality levels gated by an exponentially rising patent cost, with input quality capping output), and logistics is most cleanly handled as a consumable "transport unit" cost per sale rather than as a map. Real geography with risk (Albion, EVE) creates the richest trade gameplay but depends on PvP or scale that a mobile tycoon lacks.

### Cited Findings
- Sim Companies quality: Q0 is default; research produces patents; 12 patents for Q1, 50 more for Q2, then 500 and 2,000 for Q3 and Q4 (fan wiki, may be dated) — [Sim Companies fandom wiki: Research](https://simcompanies.fandom.com/wiki/Research)
- Sim Companies: a product's quality is capped by its lowest-quality input plus one — [Sim Companies fandom wiki: Research](https://simcompanies.fandom.com/wiki/Research)
- Sim Companies: higher-quality end products sell faster and for higher prices at retail — [Sim Companies beginner guide (ProBoards copy)](https://simcompanies.proboards.com/thread/617/original-guide-beginners)
- Sim Companies CTO executive raises the chance research converts to patents — [Executives guide](https://simcompanies.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GUIDES/pages/3080196/Executives+guide)
- Sim Companies logistics: selling on the exchange consumes transport units from your warehouse per item; contracts need half as many — [Sim Companies FAQ](https://simcompanies.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GUIDES/pages/1540097/Frequently+asked+questions)
- Sim Companies has no location model in the sources found; admin overhead scales cost with company size and is reduced by the COO — [Executives guide](https://simcompanies.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GUIDES/pages/3080196/Executives+guide)
- Albion: hauling between local city markets is the logistics game; "a transport that arrives is profitable, one that is intercepted is a total loss"; safe routes with smaller margins can beat dangerous ones; escorted guild transports are harder to intercept; predictable routes get hunted — [GuildOrder: Transport and Cargo Risk](https://guildorder.com/games/albion/wiki/transport-and-cargo-risk)
- EVE: inter-regional trade generally offers better spreads than station trading (2016 forum view, older) — [EVE forums archive](https://forums-archive.eveonline.com/topic/501794)
- Prosperous Universe: first-settler advantage on the best planets; newcomers may be pushed to weaker planets; early cooperation is blocked because logistics modules need components that take weeks to obtain (community threads) — [PrUn forum: plot system](https://com.prosperousuniverse.com/t/disadvantages-of-the-plot-system/5529); [PrUn forum: LMs in the reset universe](https://com.prosperousuniverse.com/t/lms-in-the-reset-universe/2861)
- Capitalism Lab: freight cost is a core competitive challenge alongside pricing (2025 review) — [legacyofgames](https://legacyofgames.com/2025/11/13/capitalism-lab/)
- Ticaret Simülatör includes logistics firms with trucks as a business type — [App Store TR](https://apps.apple.com/tr/app/ticaret-sim%C3%BClat%C3%B6r/id1611741960)

### Inferences
- "Input quality caps output quality plus one" forces whole-chain cooperation or vertical integration and gives veterans a durable but not absolute edge; it is the single most borrowable quality rule.
- Abstracting transport as a produced, tradable consumable (Sim Companies) gives logistics an economic weight and creates a product category without any map, routing UI or travel timers, which suits mobile sessions.
- Scarce prime locations (Prosperous Universe plots) create a permanent first-mover advantage that later players resent; if Ticarium uses cities or regions, location bonuses should be rentable, rotating or seasonal rather than permanently owned. This is inference from the PrUn complaints.

### Gaps
- Virtonomics technology, qualification and geography (countries, regions, cities, customs duties) mechanics: the game is known for them but no English source surfaced with detail.
- Transport Fever style logistics and AirlineSim route economics: no detail retrieved.
- Township, Hay Day, RichMan: no quality or logistics systems documented in sources found.

## 3. What cooperative or competitive structures exist (alliances, co-ops, seasons, resets, government/elections) and how do they affect retention?

### Takeaway
Casual titles drive retention with a weekly, league-based group race that caps per-member contribution (Township Regatta, Hay Day Derby); economy sims rely on persistent worlds with parallel realms, optional personal resets, leaderboards and, in Virtonomics, political office. No source quantified the retention effect of any of these.

### Cited Findings
- Township Regatta: co-op vs co-op race; per-member task cap of 7-15 by league; extra tasks purchasable with premium currency; chests as rewards — [Playrix help](https://playrix.helpshift.com/hc/en/3-township/faq/9251-لوحة-مهام-سباقات-الزوارق/?p=all)
- Third-party guide advises prioritising Regatta tasks over airplane orders when the race is close (vendor blog, low reliability) — [lootbar.gg](https://lootbar.gg/blog/en/township-factory-guide.html)
- Hay Day Derby: neighbourhoods matched against comparable ones, shared task board, recurring and time-limited — [myfarm.site](https://myfarm.site/articles/hay-day-derby-events-guide/)
- Hay Day Derby criticism: a player claims it is rare to win top rewards without spending diamonds to reshuffle tasks (anecdotal forum post, older) — [Supercell forum](https://forum.supercell.com/showthread.php/1933367-Is-HayDay-turning-completely-into-a-pay-to-win-game)
- Sim Companies: persistent realms, no global wipe; weekly economy-phase change every Friday that players discuss in chat; leaderboard shows the top 30 plus your own rank — [Sim Companies Economy Model](https://simcompanies.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GUIDES/pages/7536669/Economy+model); [Leveling guide](https://guides.simcompanies.com/books/leveling/export/html)
- Sim Companies personal reset: "Start Over" (from level 30) was removed; current "Reset" is available above level 5 and keeps supporter status, Sim Boosts, certificates, collectibles and notes; a "Reset" certificate is granted at level 20+ — [Reset and Start Over changes](https://simcompanies.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GUIDES/pages/2128248843/Reset+and+Start+Over+changes)
- Sim Companies opened a second realm (Entrepreneurs) rather than wiping the first; players asked for a realm with no Sim Boost purchases (older) — [Sim Companies Times #213](https://www.simcompanies.com/newspaper/0/213/)
- Virtonomics: players can be elected president of a country; long-running player competitions and auctions are cited as positives — [Virtonomics site](https://virtonomics.com/); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/virtonomics.com)
- Virtonomics offers a "Fast" realm alongside regular one-turn-per-day realms — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtonomics)
- Prosperous Universe: eight universes so far; the Early Access launch in February 2021 came with a reset pitched as a shared starting point for veterans and newcomers — [Games Press](https://www.gamespress.com/PROSPEROUS-UNIVERSE-BEGINS-EARLY-ACCESS-PHASE); [PrUn devlog #267](https://prosperousuniverse.com/blog/2021/01/11/did-someone-say-reset-267)
- AirlineSim: multiple worlds with different durations, free short worlds and paid longer or unlimited ones; new temporary and unlimited worlds planned over the coming years — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2715810/AirlineSim/)
- Albion: guild-organised escorted transports turn risky hauling into steadier income — [GuildOrder](https://guildorder.com/games/albion/wiki/transport-and-cargo-risk)
- Ticaret Simülatör: status ranks ("Baron", "Whale") from asset totals and joint-stock companies as a co-investment structure — [App Store TR](https://apps.apple.com/tr/app/ticaret-sim%C3%BClat%C3%B6r/id1611741960)

### Inferences
- The Regatta pattern (weekly cadence, league tiers, per-member task cap, small team) is the most proven co-op retention device in the set and maps naturally onto a trade game as weekly "holding" or "chamber of commerce" contracts. The per-member cap is the important detail: it limits whale carry and forces broad participation.
- Economy sims avoid global wipes on the main world and instead add parallel realms or timed worlds (Sim Companies, AirlineSim, Virtonomics Fast). This offers fresh-start competition without destroying veteran investment; it is a lower-risk form of "seasons" for Ticarium.
- A scheduled, visible macro event (Sim Companies' Friday phase roll) creates a weekly appointment and shared conversation at almost no content cost.
- Elections and government roles (Virtonomics) exist as differentiators but no source showed players valuing them; treat as unproven.

### Gaps
- No retention data (D1/D7/D30) for any title; no source linking a specific social feature to retention numbers.
- Virtonomics election and tender mechanics not documented in sources found.
- No detail on Sim Companies alliances or group structures; EVE corporations and Albion guild seasons not researched in depth.
- Season lengths: only the weekly cadence of Sim Companies phases is confirmed; Regatta and Derby exact durations were not confirmed in sources retrieved.

## 4. How do they onboard new players and prevent veterans from dominating?

### Takeaway
Weak onboarding is the most consistent complaint across deep economy sims (Virtonomics, Prosperous Universe, and Ticarium itself). The anti-domination tools that appear in sources are: level-gated feature unlocks, overhead that scales with size, market-share soft limits, matchmaking by league, parallel fresh realms, and guided post-tutorial contracts.

### Cited Findings
- Sim Companies gates complexity by level: exchange and contracts at level 5, executives at level 15 — [apkfunblog tutorial](https://sites.google.com/view/apkfunblog/blog/tutorials-for-sim-companies-beginner); [Executives guide](https://simcompanies.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GUIDES/pages/3080196/Executives+guide)
- Sim Companies maintains official guides (beginner guide, FAQ, economy model, executives, bonds, aerospace, leveling) and a player-written newspaper inside the game — [Sim Companies Guides overview](https://simcompanies.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GUIDES/overview); [Sim Companies Times](https://www.simcompanies.com/library/0/newspaper/390/)
- Sim Companies administration overhead grows with company size and is offset by executives, acting as a diseconomy of scale — [Executives guide](https://simcompanies.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GUIDES/pages/3080196/Executives+guide)
- Sim Companies forum answer: the game is designed so that even a player with unlimited real money cannot rank first in a week — [Sim Companies Q&A](https://www.simcompanies.com/articles/q-and-a-session/)
- Virtonomics: multiple reviewers report no clear introduction or walkthrough and a hard-to-navigate UI; wiki and help largely in Russian; slow support — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/virtonomics.com?page=5)
- Virtonomics market-share heuristic of about 15% maximum per market for sustainable operation (player advice) — [Virtonomics forum](https://virtonomics.com/mary/forum/forum_new/13/topic/105136/view)
- Prosperous Universe added post-tutorial contracts to walk new players to a second base — [Steam news](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1761220/allnews/)
- Prosperous Universe veterans hold the best planets; a developer reply says they consider future universes when changing plot rules — [PrUn forum](https://com.prosperousuniverse.com/t/disadvantages-of-the-plot-system/5529)
- Albion: finishing the tutorial grants 3 days of premium status — [Albion Free Market beginner guide 2025](https://www.albionfreemarket.com/articles/view/albion-online-beginner-s-guide-2025)
- Hay Day Derby and Township Regatta match groups against comparable groups or by league — [myfarm.site](https://myfarm.site/articles/hay-day-derby-events-guide/); [Playrix help](https://playrix.helpshift.com/hc/en/3-township/faq/9251-لوحة-مهام-سباقات-الزوارق/?p=all)
- Ticarium reviewers report no tutorial and difficulty getting past the first shop; developer blocks player money transfers to prevent pay-to-win — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ticarium&hl=en_US); [App Store TR](https://apps.apple.com/tr/app/ticarium-online-ticaret-oyunu/id1620118127?l=tr)

### Inferences
- A trial of the premium tier at tutorial completion (Albion) is a cheap onboarding and conversion device that also shows new players what efficient play feels like.
- Size-scaling overhead (Sim Companies) is better received than flat caps: Ticarium players complain about a hard cap of 15 facilities and about taxes, whereas no comparable complaint about Sim Companies admin overhead surfaced in the sources found (absence of evidence, not proof of acceptance). Framing matters: a cost players can reduce through a system (executives) reads as gameplay, while a tax reads as punishment.
- Blocking all player-to-player transfers (Ticarium) solves pay-to-win and multi-account funnelling but removes contracts, the feature Sim Companies uses to build relationships. Fee-free but price-banded goods contracts would be a middle path. This is inference.
- Guided "next goal" contracts after the tutorial (Prosperous Universe) target the known drop-off point where a sandbox opens up.

### Gaps
- No tutorial completion or early-churn numbers for any title.
- EVE's new-player experience and anti-domination tools (high-sec, NPC corps) were not retrieved.
- Whether Sim Companies still limits one company per realm and how it polices multi-accounting was not confirmed for 2025-2026.

## 5. How do they monetize, and which approaches are perceived as fair vs pay-to-win?

### Takeaway
Perceived-fair models are: a flat subscription or licence that adds convenience and information (Sim Companies supporter charts, Albion premium, Prosperous Universe PRO, AirlineSim credits), premium currency that is also earnable and cannot buy speed, and opt-in rewarded ads. Perceived-unfair models are: moving core economic features behind premium currency after the fact (Virtonomics), forced interstitial ads (RichMan), paid extra competition tasks or reshuffles (Regatta, Derby), and cash-to-currency exchanges (Albion gold, EVE PLEX), which remain contested.

### Cited Findings
- Sim Companies: Sim Boosts are the premium currency, earned from daily login and producing/selling or bought with real money; in-game cash cannot buy them; a moderator says they cannot buy speed bonuses, only realign bonuses and cosmetics such as HQ designs; "plenty available for free, most winners did not purchase any" (older) — [Sim Companies Times #213](https://www.simcompanies.com/newspaper/0/213/); [Sim Companies Q&A](https://www.simcompanies.com/articles/q-and-a-session/)
- Sim Companies beginner guide mentions buying Exchange Sim Boosts with in-game money up to $10,000 a day, which conflicts with the statement that cash cannot buy Sim Boosts (different dates or a different mechanism; unresolved) — [Sim Companies beginner guide](https://simcompanies.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GUIDES/pages/196609/Guide+for+beginners); contradicted by [Sim Companies Times #213](https://www.simcompanies.com/newspaper/0/213/)
- Sim Companies supporter tier gives market analytics (price history charts, per-quality candlesticks, VWAP) — [Steam community hub](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2253720)
- Sim Companies Steam reviews are split: some say purchases are optional and not compulsory, others call it pay-to-win and a "shady cash grab" that changes rules without warning — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2253720/Sim_Companies)
- Virtonomics: the most frequent complaint is pay-to-win and paywalls; reviewers say Game Points are needed to progress and that auctions, bonds and structures were moved behind paywalls; one calls it "pay-to-progress" rather than pay-to-win; others say efficiency requirements are hard to meet without a paid subscription; positives include no ads — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/virtonomics.com)
- Prosperous Universe: three tiers (FREE, BASIC after paying for at least one month of PRO, PRO monthly subscription); developers say PRO lets you play at a different speed and is not pay-to-win; community debates this — [PrUn handbook: Licenses](https://handbook.apex.prosperousuniverse.com/tutorials/current-tutorials/06-licenses/index.html); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1761220/discussions/0/6126615404778658094)
- Prosperous Universe 2025 gifting rules: licence gifts may not be exchanged for in-game valuables or real money, because the developers do not want players with a big real-world purse buying in-game advantages from other players — [PrUn devlog #472](https://prosperousuniverse.com/blog/2025/02/25/gifting-rules-472)
- Albion: premium can be bought with in-game silver via gold; the silver-gold exchange is the main thing players call pay-to-win; a 30% fee on gold-market purchases was reported as an anti-RMT measure (undated); ItemLevel calls it between "pay-to-progress" and "pay-to-lose"; developers once said pay-to-win shop items are "a no-go" (older) — [Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/general/news/is-albion-online-pay-to-win); [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/mmo/gold-markets-albion-online-purchase-fees-trading); [ItemLevel](https://itemlevel.net/is-albion-online-pay-to-win/); [Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/albion-online-is-getting-more-expensive-developers-say-because-the-mmorpg-is-now-better,465286)
- EVE: a third-party analysis of the January 2026 economic report puts PLEX-related trades at a very large share of market value (blogger calculation, not CCP framing); PLEX average daily price fell about 20% from the global PLEX market's creation in July 2025 to year end — [The Nosy Gamer, Feb 2026](https://nosygamer.blogspot.com/2026/02/a-different-look-at-eve-onlines-monthly.html); [The Nosy Gamer, Jan 2026](https://nosygamer.blogspot.com/2026/01/eve-onlines-december-2025-monthly.html)
- AirlineSim: explicitly not free-to-play; no in-app purchases for advantage or cosmetics; short worlds free forever; longer worlds cost credits charged daily; one completed payment unlocks player-interaction features — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2715810/AirlineSim/)
- Hay Day: IAP-led with meaningful ad revenue and a recently added subscription; diamonds used for upgrades, items and speed-ups, especially in derby and boat systems; lifetime revenue about $1.2B (Udonis) or about $1.4B as of spring 2026 (Appmagic via cellstring) — [Udonis](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/hay-day-monetization); [cellstring](https://cellstring.com/news/article/how-hay-day-continues-to-make-millions-after-14-years); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/game-monetization-design-analysis-of-hay-day)
- Hay Day is described as "extremely non-payer friendly" by Supercell's product lead and as having no hard pay-to-win elements, while a forum thread argues the Derby is pay-to-win — [cellstring](https://cellstring.com/news/article/how-hay-day-continues-to-make-millions-after-14-years); contradicted by [Supercell forum](https://forum.supercell.com/showthread.php/1933367-Is-HayDay-turning-completely-into-a-pay-to-win-game)
- Township: T-cash premium currency for speed-ups on trains, airplanes, helicopter orders and regatta tasks, plus buying extra regatta tasks — [Township wiki mirror: Speed Up](https://breezewiki.discard.no/township/wiki/Speed_Up); [Playrix help](https://playrix.helpshift.com/hc/en/3-township/faq/9251-لوحة-مهام-سباقات-الزوارق/?p=all)
- Business Empire: RichMan: reviews flag aggressive forced ad frequency that interrupts play and progress loss without cloud sync — [marlvel.ai](https://marlvel.ai/api/llm/apps/games/com-xentech-empire)
- Idle Miner Tycoon: rewarded-video model, opt-in for offline bonuses and boosts; players still complain about launch pop-ups (secondary summaries) — [marlvel.ai](https://marlvel.ai/intel-report/games/idle-miner-tycoon-gold-digger)
- Capitalism Lab: premium PC title, bundles $29.99-$39.99 with DLC around $10 each — [Capitalism Lab](https://www.capitalismlab.com/)
- Ticarium: free with IAP and ads; a player describes ads as mostly rewarded and not excessive; licence fees and taxes are the main economy complaints — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ticarium&hl=en_US)

### Inferences
- Selling information and convenience (charts, analytics, extra order slots, automation) is the best-tolerated monetisation in player-economy games, because it does not inject goods or currency into the shared market.
- The fastest route to "pay-to-win" backlash is retroactively paywalling an economic feature that used to be free (Virtonomics) or selling extra actions in a group competition (Regatta tasks, Derby reshuffles).
- Ticarium's rewarded-ads approach is already perceived as fair; the reputational risk lies in sinks (taxes, licences) that feel like pressure to buy currency. Converting those sinks into reducible, system-driven costs would lower that risk.
- Any real-money-to-currency bridge needs anti-RMT friction (Albion's fee, PrUn's gifting rules); Ticarium's ban on transfers is the strictest version of this.

- Deconstructor of Fun and GameRefinery analyses could not be read in full (host blocked), so Township's monetisation breakdown is thin.
- EVE PLEX share figure is from a single blogger and was returned by search with ambiguous wording; verify before citing.

### Inferences

- Design shortlist for the report writer, derived from the sections above. Borrow: hybrid pricing (player market plus saturation-based retail sink plus weekly macro phase); input-capped quality with steeply rising research cost; transport as a consumable; fee-free contracts as a relationship tool; executives or staff as the way to reduce size-scaling overhead; weekly league co-op race with per-member task caps; parallel fresh realms instead of wipes; tutorial-end premium trial; guided post-tutorial goals; paid analytics. Avoid: hard price caps without discovery; daily manual re-pricing chores with no automation; retroactive paywalls on economic features; forced interstitial ads; paid extra competition actions; permanent ownership of scarce prime locations; flat facility caps and tax framing for sinks.
