# Economy-sim design principles for a mobile multiplayer business/trade tycoon (reference: Ticarium: Business Tycoon)

Research date: 2026-11-13. Method note: direct page fetching was blocked for most hosts (support.google.com failed with ENOTFOUND; only developer.apple.com was fetched in full). Everything else rests on web-search result summaries, so quotes from those pages are second-hand and should be re-checked against the live page before being relied on. Source quality is marked per finding: [primary], [academic], [vendor blog], [aggregator]. No number here is invented; where sources disagree both are given.

## 1. Virtual economy fundamentals: faucets/sinks, inflation, price discovery, documented failures

### Takeaway
Inflation is controlled by sinks that destroy value (fees, taxes, upkeep, failure), not by re-pricing; live games that run player markets (EVE, OSRS) publish and tune faucet/sink totals continuously, and the best-documented failures (Diablo 3 auction house, thin player-only markets) come from letting trading replace the core loop or from having no liquidity backstop. For a small studio the evidence favours a hybrid: NPC/"market-maker" price floors and ceilings around a taxed player market, rather than a pure order book.

### Cited Findings
- Faucets are every source of currency (rewards, quests, rewarded ads); sinks are every place it is removed (upgrades, purchases, fees). When sources outpace sinks currency loses value; when sinks are too aggressive players feel starved and disengage. [vendor blog] — [Qyren: Faucets and Sinks guide for indie studios](https://www.qyren.ai/blog/faucets-sinks-guide); [Game Growth Advisor, 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-07-14-game-economy-design-virtual-currency-balancing/)
- Practitioner advice: design sinks before rewards (map spending across the player lifecycle first), and "add sinks before you adjust pricing — pricing changes rarely fix inflation". Track inflation rate and a sink-to-faucet ratio (currency removed / currency introduced per period or progression tier). [vendor blog] — [Qyren](https://www.qyren.ai/blog/faucets-sinks-guide)
- "Hard" sinks that destroy value are the real inflation tools: NPC consumable purchases, repair fees, auction listing costs, crafting failure that burns materials. [low-authority Medium post] — [M. Sahin, Designing Game Economies](https://medium.com/@msahinn21/designing-game-economies-inflation-resource-management-and-balance-fa1e6c894670)
- Unity publishes a game economy design guide covering IAP, hybrid and D2C; it was the most authoritative of the general guides found but is short on failure cases. [vendor, primary] — [Unity: Game Economy Design guide](https://unity.com/resources/game-economy-design-guide)
- EVE Online: CCP publishes a Monthly Economic Report with ISK faucet and sink totals. March 2026: total ISK faucets rose 17%, with a new commodity faucet ("EDENCOM Data") entering the top six; April 2026: faucets fell back to February levels. [primary] — [CCP MER March 2026](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/monthly-economic-report-march-2026); [CCP MER April 2026](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/monthly-economic-report-april-2026)
- EVE: a limited-time event (Crimson Harvest, Oct 2025) produced a marked jump in commodity faucets because event items could be turned in for ISK — events are faucet shocks. [community analysis] — [TAGN on the October 2025 MER](https://tagn.wordpress.com/2025/11/14/the-october-2025-eve-online-monthly-economic-report/)
- EVE: third-party analysis of January 2026 data put ISK faucets at 203.6 trillion; and a blogger found the November 2025 report's sinks/faucets chart off by 9% — even a mature studio's economy telemetry contains errors. [community analysis] — [Nosy Gamer, Jan 2026](https://nosygamer.blogspot.com/2026/02/a-different-look-at-eve-onlines-monthly.html); [Nosy Gamer, Nov 2025](https://nosygamer.blogspot.com/2025/12/eve-onlines-november-2025-monthly.html)
- Old School RuneScape: the Grand Exchange charges a tax on most trades, raised from 1% to 2% on 29 May 2025, capped at 5 million coins per item. Part of the tax funds a coffer that buys items from player orders and deletes them (an item sink). Daily quotas are not published. [wiki + primary announcement] — [OSRS Wiki: Grand Exchange](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Grand_Exchange); [Jagex: Grand Exchange Tax & Item Sink](https://secure.runescape.com/m=news/grand-exchange-tax--item-sink?oldschool=1)
- Academic evaluation of that intervention: the tax "did not meaningfully affect the trading volume of items at the tax boundaries", while the item sink contributed to inflation of luxury-good prices. Gold sinks are deflationary, item sinks inflationary for the items removed, so the net effect of mixed interventions can be unclear. [academic, 2022] — [Hogan-Hennessy, Market Interventions in a Large-Scale Virtual Economy (arXiv 2210.07970)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.07970); [OSRS Wiki: Economy](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Economy)
- Diablo 3 (older classic, 2013-14): Blizzard announced in Sept 2013 that it would close both the gold and real-money auction houses (shut 18 March 2014), stating the system undermined Diablo's core gameplay; former director Jay Wilson said it "really hurt the game". Retrospective criticism: buying the best gear made playing feel redundant. [trade press / primary] — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/blizzard-to-shut-down-i-diablo-3-i-auction-house); [Blizzard announcement](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/10974978/diablo-iii-auction-house-update); [GameSpot retrospective](https://gamespot.com/articles/ten-years-later-lessons-from-diablo-iiis-auction-house-disaster-have-not-been-remembered/1100-6503489/)
- Gaia Online is cited (one source only, unverified) as a case where a cash-shop item granting random gold flooded the economy — real-money faucets need tight limits. [single low-authority source] — [M. Sahin, Medium](https://medium.com/@msahinn21/designing-game-economies-inflation-resource-management-and-balance-fa1e6c894670)
- Prosperous Universe (browser economy MMO): all commodities are supplied by players, prices set on real-time exchanges; but faction-controlled "market makers" post fixed-price buy/sell orders, and beginners depend on selling into those buy orders to keep their loop going. [developer press kit + player guide] — [simulogics press kit](https://press.simulogics.games/prosperousuniverse/); [Steam beginner guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3545186148)
- An early-access preview of Prosperous Universe (c. 2019, older) observed trade slowing after the early phase and the economy seeming to need a constant inflow of capital from new players. Single observation, not a study. [press preview] — [MMORPG.com preview](https://www.mmorpg.com/previews/prosperous-universe-preview-prosperity-comes-through-complexity-2000108138)
- Virtonomics markets itself on conditions being the outcome of every player's choices; a third-party ranking notes prices crash when too many players flood one commodity. [store listing / aggregator] — [Virtonomics on App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/virtonomics/id6450962658); [supplychaindisaster.com ranking](https://www.supplychaindisaster.com/best-business-simulation-games)

### Inferences
- Order book vs AMM vs NPC curve, for a small team: a pure player order book needs population per item per region to function (Prosperous Universe needed NPC market makers even on PC). A launch-safe design is an NPC price curve that moves with aggregate supply/demand (acts as buyer and seller of last resort, with a spread that is itself a sink), with a taxed player-to-player market layered on once DAU supports it. NPC bands also cap manipulation and make prices legible to new players.
- The spread between NPC buy and NPC sell prices, plus a transaction tax, gives two tunable sinks that scale with activity; the OSRS paper suggests a 1-2% tax does not suppress volume, so it is a low-risk lever.
- Events must be budgeted as faucets (EVE evidence). Every event reward should ship with a matching sink or be paid in a non-tradable currency.
- Rewarded ads are a faucet. If ad rewards pay tradable soft currency, ad watching inflates the shared economy; safer to pay them in time-skips, non-tradable boosts or capped amounts.
- The Diablo lesson for tycoon games is the inverse: trading is the core loop here, so the risk is not "market replaces play" but "paying replaces trading skill". Keep purchasable currency out of the price-setting market or cap it.
- Build faucet/sink telemetry from day one (per-source currency created/destroyed per day); it is the one dashboard every mature economy game has.

### Gaps
- No primary Lehdonvirta & Castronova (Virtual Economies, MIT Press 2014) text was retrieved; it is the standard reference but could not be quoted here.
- No source found comparing order-book vs AMM/bonding-curve pricing specifically in games; the AMM recommendation above is inference.
- Albion Online, New World (gold duplication/deflation) and Sim Companies developer write-ups were searched but nothing usable came back.
- No data on Ticarium's own economy implementation was collected in this task.

## 2. What makes tycoon decisions interesting rather than a wait-and-tap checklist

### Takeaway
Sourced evidence here is thin and mostly from practitioner material: the consistent points are that the meta loop needs genuine choices where no option is always optimal, short goal cadence early, and scarcity created by other players' behaviour (flooded markets crash prices). Most of the specific mechanic recommendations below are design inference rather than measured results.

### Cited Findings
- Machinations' idle-game guide: to keep the meta loop engaging players must be given "genuinely meaningful choices"; it concedes not every choice can be deep (some can be cosmetic) and stresses an economy sophisticated enough to keep giving reasons to spend currency. [vendor blog] — [Machinations: How to design idle games](https://machinations.io/articles/idle-games-and-how-to-design-them)
- A Machinations community idle/tycoon diagram sets the target "meaningful small goals every 30-90 seconds" early on, and models a higher-effort action paying roughly 2x but "not always strictly optimal because of time and fatigue". [community example, low authority] — [Machinations community diagram](https://machinations.io/community/wtf.anarh1st/create-an-idletycoon-cd7bb7537c8211f190390abc5ce0dcc9)
- In player-driven business sims, oversupply by many players crashes a commodity's price, i.e. the best choice depends on what others do. [aggregator] — [supplychaindisaster.com](https://www.supplychaindisaster.com/best-business-simulation-games)
- Prosperous Universe positions complexity (production chains, logistics between locations, exchanges) as the source of depth; the same preview flags that this complexity is also its onboarding cost. [press preview, older] — [MMORPG.com preview](https://www.mmorpg.com/previews/prosperous-universe-preview-prosperity-comes-through-complexity-2000108138)
- Diablo 3's auction house shows a decision system can be removed by a shortcut: once the optimal action was "buy it", the play loop felt redundant. [trade press] — [GameSpot retrospective](https://gamespot.com/articles/ten-years-later-lessons-from-diablo-iiis-auction-house-disaster-have-not-been-remembered/1100-6503489/)

### Inferences
- A checklist appears when every upgrade is affordable eventually and none excludes another. Interesting decisions need at least one of: mutually exclusive investment (limited slots/land/licences), opportunity cost on a shared scarce input (capital, storage, transport capacity), or payoffs that depend on other players.
- Mechanics most likely to produce this at low content cost: (a) specialization with switching cost, so comparative advantage and trade between players emerge; (b) city/region price differences with transport time and capacity, giving arbitrage and routing choices; (c) demand that saturates per good per region, so copying the "best" build lowers its return; (d) telegraphed events (shortage, tax change, demand spike) that reward preparation, with variance small enough that it does not read as gambling; (e) perishable or storage-limited goods to force sell-now vs hold.
- Uncertainty should come from other players and announced events more than from hidden random rolls; this also keeps the game clear of gambling-like framing (see section 6).
- Each added system increases the simulation the server must validate; spatial logistics and saturating demand are cheap (formula-driven), full production chains with dozens of goods are expensive in balancing time.

### Gaps
- No Lost Garden (Daniel Cook) essay or GDC talk on this topic was retrieved; Sid Meier's "interesting decisions" framing is well known but was not sourced here.
- No quantitative evidence (A/B data) was found linking specialization, logistics or events to retention in tycoon games.
- No teardown of Ticarium or direct competitors (e.g. Sim Companies, Idle/Business Empire titles) by Deconstructor of Fun, Naavik or GameRefinery was found.

## 3. First-time user experience and retention benchmarks

### Takeaway
Across all mobile games the 2025 median is roughly D1 22%, D7 under 4%, D30 under 1% (GameAnalytics, via secondary summaries); a widely repeated simulation-genre table says D1 30.1% / D7 8.7% / D30 3.0% but its primary source is unclear and another source gives far lower numbers. Tutorial completion above about 80% is treated as healthy by practitioners; for complex sims the recommended pattern is staged disclosure over several sessions, measured as a step funnel.

### Cited Findings
- GameAnalytics' 2026 Mobile & PC Gaming Benchmarks (2025 data, 16,000+ mobile games with at least 1,000 monthly users): median D1 about 22%, D7 just under 4%, D30 about 0.7-0.8%; median daily playtime about 12 minutes. Reported via secondary summaries; the report itself was not opened. — [GameAnalytics report page](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2026-mobile-pc-gaming-benchmarks); [Segwise summary](https://segwise.ai/blog/mobile-gaming-app-user-retention-strategies); [AppFollow summary](https://appfollow.io/blog/mobile-game-retention)
- Top-quartile D1 in the same report: 31-33% on iOS vs 25-27% on Android. — [Innovecs summary](https://www.innovecsgames.com/blog/mobile-game-retention-benchmarks/)
- The 2026 GameAnalytics edition reportedly does not publish genre-level benchmarks because of genre re-categorisation. — [Galaxy4Games summary](https://galaxy4games.com/en/knowledgebase/blog/mobile-game-retention-benchmarks-2026-d1-d7-d30-data-for-publishers)
- Simulation genre: D1 30.10%, D7 8.71%, D30 2.96% appears in several blog tables without a clear primary source. [unverified] — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/mobile-gaming-app-user-retention-strategies); [Zorka](https://zorka.agency/blog/13-kpis-to-measure-your-mobile-game); [Digital Minds BPO](https://digitalmindsbpo.com/blog/mobile-game-retention-benchmarks/)
- Conflicting lower figure: simulation average D7 2.4%, D30 0.50% (attributed to Mistplay; methodology unclear). — [maf.ad rewarded ads stats](https://maf.ad/en/blog/rewarded-ads-stats/)
- A claim that simulation games "can achieve 45-60% D1 and 20-30% D30" appears unsourced and should be discounted. — [Juego Studio](https://www.juegostudio.com/blog/how-to-increase-user-retention-and-increase-your-games-lifetime)
- Tutorial completion: above 80% strong; 65-80% acceptable, worth A/B testing; below 65% critical. Another guide: under 70% means wasting a large share of UA spend. [vendor blogs] — [Playio: Onboarding decides your D1](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-onboarding-retention); [devtodev FTUE glossary](https://www.devtodev.com/resources/glossary/ftue-first-time-user-experience)
- Measure the tutorial as a per-step funnel: "12% of users leave at step three" is more useful than "completion is 70%". — [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-onboarding-retention)
- For complex genres, reveal systems across about three mini-sessions rather than one long tutorial, and A/B test all-up-front vs staggered. Sandbox onboarding suits strategy/building games but can overwhelm beginners and costs more to build. [practitioner blogs] — [Adrian Crook: Best practices for mobile game onboarding](https://adriancrook.com/best-practices-for-mobile-game-onboarding/); [Adrian Crook: Reduce drop-off during onboarding](https://adriancrook.com/reduce-drop-off-during-onboarding/)
- Optimising only the tutorial can miss D2-D7 problems; onboarding should be treated as the whole first week. — [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-onboarding-retention)

### Inferences
- Planning targets for an Android-first tycoon: D1 at or above 27-30% (Android top quartile is 25-27%), D7 in the 5-9% range, D30 2-3% would be good; anything near the all-games median (22 / 4 / 0.8) means the game is not yet worth scaling. These are derived from the conflicting figures above, not a published simulation benchmark.
- Staged unlock order that fits the genre: session 1 = one business, buy-produce-sell to an NPC at a visible profit; session 2 = second good and a price difference between two places; session 3+ = player market, then guild/season. The player market and anything with other players' prices should be gated until the player has made several profitable NPC trades.
- Log every tutorial step as an analytics event before launch; with AdMob/Firebase already in use, Firebase Analytics funnels cover this at no extra cost.

### Gaps
- No verified primary simulation/tycoon-genre retention benchmark for 2025-2026 was found; GameAnalytics' report must be opened directly.
- No genre-specific tutorial completion benchmark was found; thresholds above are generic.
- A claimed GameAnalytics 2025 link between FTUE completion and 2x D1 could not be verified and is not relied on.

## 4. Seasons/resets, leagues, catch-up mechanics, guilds: effect on retention and fairness

### Takeaway
The evidence that these structures help is mostly design analysis rather than measured lift: season passes give free and paying players a recurring reason to log in, resets give lapsed and late players a re-entry point, and guilds help retention in some games but not all. No controlled data quantifying the uplift was found.

### Cited Findings
- Deconstructor of Fun (2022): battle passes give everyone an incentive to play regularly through the free track; the retention effect is stronger for buyers (unused purchase is wasted); passes turn the game into a hobby kept for months; aggressive monetisation risks burning players. [design analysis] — [Deconstructor of Fun: Battle Pass Design](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2022/6/4/battle-passes-analysis)
- Deconstructor of Fun (2018, older): Fortnite's pass is both a conversion item and a retention driver, and being limited to a season is what makes it compelling. — [Deconstructor of Fun: Fortnite](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2018/4/21/aegai526kyjvsn69xm6uc2x02we48x)
- In Clash Royale and Brawl Stars, season resets reduce trophies in the top leagues, start a new pass and usually add some content and rebalancing. — [Deconstructor of Fun: Clash Mini](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2022/1/14/clash-mini)
- Heroic Labs argues the reset itself helps retention by giving lapsed players "a clean moment to return". [vendor blog] — [Heroic Labs: What is a battle pass](https://heroiclabs.com/blog/what-is-a-battle-pass-in-a-live-service-game/)
- Self-reported anecdote from a former League of Legends growth team member: a simplified pass plus new-player bundle beat KPIs for short- and long-term retention and payer conversion; XP boosts helped new players catch up with advanced friends. [anecdote, no data] — [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/davebruno_having-user-end-experience-with-battle-passes-activity-7090493133322424321-QW0H)
- Academic: survival analysis of 1,204 MMORPG users found both guild and friend systems increase retention, the guild system more strongly. PC MMORPG, so transfer to mobile tycoon is uncertain. — [Hwang & Han, SSRN 4606486](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4606486)
- Academic: in two commercial F2P mobile games, adding social features did not necessarily improve retention; early social engagement predicted D30 retention in one game but not the other. — [University of Jyväskylä thesis](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/66822)
- Academic (2025): Korean mobile game players often feel compelled to keep up with guild members or peers, reinforcing sunk-cost spending — guild pressure is also a monetisation and fairness risk. — [Playing to Pay (arXiv 2504.10714)](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10714v1)
- The frequently quoted "guild members have 2-3x higher D30 retention" has no primary study behind it in the sources found. [unverified] — [Hubapps blog](https://hubapps.team/blog/mobile-game-retention-strategies)

### Inferences
- A persistent compounding economy without resets structurally favours early joiners and payers; this is the main fairness complaint in tycoon leaderboards. Seasonal leagues that rank on growth during the season (percentage or season-only score) rather than lifetime net worth give late joiners a real chance without wiping progress.
- Lowest-risk structure for a small team: permanent "career" progress (cosmetics, titles, small permanent unlocks) plus a 4-8 week season layer with its own leaderboard brackets grouped by start date or tier, and a free+paid pass tied to it. A soft reset of season currency also acts as a periodic inflation sink (links to section 1).
- Catch-up options with low abuse risk: bracketed leagues, newcomer protection windows, and cheaper early upgrades as the server ages. XP/earnings boosts sold for money are catch-up for some players and pay-to-win for others, so prefer granting them free by account age.
- Guilds: build small cooperative goals (shared contracts, joint supply orders) before competitive guild wars; the academic evidence says the effect is real but game-dependent, so ship a minimal version and measure D30 by guild membership.

### Gaps
- No controlled or published quantitative lift (retention delta) for battle passes, seasons, leagues or catch-up mechanics was found.
- No evidence specific to economy/tycoon games on season resets (most examples are PvP action/strategy).
- No GameRefinery/Liftoff or Naavik feature-prevalence data for the tycoon subgenre was retrieved.

## 5. Monetization: rewarded ads, passes, cosmetics vs power, benchmarks, Turkish market

### Takeaway
No Turkey-specific eCPM or simulation-specific ARPDAU figure could be found; global Android rewarded eCPM is reported around $6.70 (TopOn, H1 2025, a three-year low) with a country range of roughly $3.3-$12.9 among the top 20 markets, and Turkey is likely at or below the low end. Turkish players spent about $347-374M on mobile game IAP in 2025 (sources disagree), prices are typically localised 60-70% below Europe, and rewarded ads plus a cheap season pass fit the market better than high-priced IAP.

### Cited Findings
- TopOn H1 2025 global report: Android rewarded video eCPM $6.70, described as a three-year low. [mediation vendor report] — [TopOn H1 2025 report (PDF)](https://mores.toponad.com/reports/TopOn%20Global%20Mobile%20Games%20Monetization%20Report%20_%202025%20H1.pdf)
- Android rewarded eCPM across the top 20 countries ranges from $3.31 to $12.91 (US: $12.91 Android, $13.18 iOS). Another source gives US Android rewarded $16.49 for Q4 2024 — sources conflict. [vendor blogs] — [Playio: eCPM benchmarks 2026](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-ecpm-benchmarks-2026); [MonetizeMore playbook](https://www.monetizemore.com/blog/admob-monetization/)
- Latin America's Android rewarded eCPM averaged around $2.00, similar to Southeast Asia — the nearest proxy found for a tier-3-priced market; it is not Turkish data. — [Undrads](https://www.undrads.com/blogs/how-much-do-mobile-games-make-per-ad)
- Appodeal's 2025 eCPM report (100,000+ apps): rewarded video leads eCPM, interstitials steady, banners lowest; iOS higher in North America/Europe, Android performs relatively well in APAC and LATAM. The country table (which may include Turkey) could not be read. — [Appodeal eCPM Report 2025 (PDF)](https://appodeal.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Appodeal-The-Latest-eCPM-Report-2025.pdf)

A "health floor" of $0.15 for ad-monetised games is also quoted. Not genre- or Turkey-specific. [vendor blogs] — [Playio: ARPDAU benchmarks](https://blog.playio.co/arpdau-benchmarks-mobile-games); [Playio: rewarded ad benchmarks 2026](https://blog.playio.co/rewarded-ad-benchmarks-2026)
- Turkey: players' mobile game IAP spending in 2025 was $347M, up 6% and a record (AppMagic via Udonis); another digest cites $374M with the same growth rate. — [Udonis](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-game-market/turkiye); [mobilegamer.biz data digest](https://mobilegamer.biz/data-digest-turkiye-us-asia-and-mena-market-stats-pokemon-go-ua-funding-and-more/)
- Turkey ranks 8th globally for downloads and app time; installs grew 12% in 2024. — [games.gg](https://games.gg/news/turkey-mobile-app-revenue-to-hit-1-billion/)
- Turkey 2025 mobile ARPU $20.51 on $899M revenue and 43.9M users (definition differs from the AppMagic IAP figure; treat with caution). — [Allcorrect Mobile Game Market Index 2025](https://allcorrectgames.com/insights/mobile-game-market-index-2025-top-30-markets-growth-trends-opportunities/)
- Localisation guidance for Turkey: price roughly 60-70% below European levels, re-check regularly because of lira depreciation; microtransactions often sit at 10-50 lira. [vendor blog, directional; lira amounts date quickly] — [Inlingo: Turkish game market overview](https://inlingogames.com/blog/the-turkish-gaming-market/)
- Turkish studios earned about $2.76B in worldwide IAP in 2025 (Dream Games about $1.7B) — the domestic market is small relative to export revenue. — [GameDev Reports: AppMagic & Gamigion](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-and-gamigion-turkiyes-mobile)
- Battle pass pricing/design principles (free track for all, paid track that only pays off through play) per Deconstructor of Fun, 2022. — [Battle Pass Design](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2022/6/4/battle-passes-analysis)
- Pay-to-win harm case: Diablo 3's real-money auction house, where buying the best gear made play redundant. — [GameSpot retrospective](https://gamespot.com/articles/ten-years-later-lessons-from-diablo-iiis-auction-house-disaster-have-not-been-remembered/1100-6503489/)

### Inferences
- Rough revenue arithmetic for a Turkey-heavy audience, clearly an estimate: if rewarded eCPM is about $2-3.5 and an engaged tycoon player watches 3-5 rewarded ads per day, ad ARPDAU is about $0.006-0.018 from rewarded alone. A 10,000 DAU game would earn roughly $60-180 per day before interstitials/IAP. This sets the ceiling on backend spend (section 7) and argues for including higher-eCPM geos via English localisation.
- In a shared-economy game, players are likely to call something pay-to-win when money (a) buys the currency that sets market prices, (b) moves leaderboard rank, or (c) removes a scarcity other players must manage. Safer paid items: cosmetics (HQ skins, company logos, titles), convenience that is capped (extra queue slot, offline-collection length), season pass with mostly cosmetic and modest capped resources, and ad removal.
- Rewarded ads should be opt-in, capped per day, and give the same capped benefit to every player, so non-payers reach parity with time; uncapped ad-for-cash becomes both an inflation faucet and a bot incentive.
- If hard currency can be converted to tradable soft currency, cap the conversion or keep purchased currency out of the player market; otherwise the market price of everything becomes a function of spend.
- A low-priced season pass (local price tier) plus ad-removal purchase is consistent with Turkish price sensitivity; subscriptions lacked any supporting evidence in this research.

### Gaps
- No Turkey-specific rewarded/interstitial eCPM for 2025-2026 was found; the Appodeal and TopOn PDFs probably contain it but could not be opened. The studio's own AdMob console for its two published games is the best available source.
- No simulation/tycoon-genre ARPDAU benchmark was found.
- No survey or study of what players label pay-to-win was found; the definition above is inference.
- No evidence on subscription performance in this genre, or on Turkish payment habits (carrier billing, card penetration, Google Play gift cards).
- GameRefinery/Liftoff, Sensor Tower and AppsFlyer genre reports were not retrieved.

## 6. Store-policy and legal risks: simulated crypto/stock trading, gambling-like mechanics, loot boxes, ratings

### Takeaway
A fictional stock or crypto market using only non-withdrawable in-game currency is not covered by Apple's crypto rules (which address real wallets, exchanges, mining and ICOs) or by the real-money gambling rules, but anything that looks like a casino mini-game, paid random rewards, or cash-out value creates real exposure: paid random items require odds disclosure on both stores, and Google has geo-blocked a game in 13 countries for content it judged to simulate gambling. Google's own policy pages could not be fetched, so wording below must be re-verified.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- A simulated exchange (fictional tickers/coins, in-game currency only, no cash-out, no real coin names or prices presented as investment guidance) falls outside the cited crypto and real-money rules. Risk rises if the game: uses real cryptocurrency or company names and logos (trademark and "financial app" misclassification), promises or implies real earnings, lets currency be transferred in ways that enable real-money trading, or frames trading as betting.
- Avoid casino-style presentation (slot reels, roulette, "double or nothing") even with virtual currency: it can force a higher age rating, exclusion from family audiences, and country geo-blocks on Google Play as in the deckbuilder case. A "lucky wheel" paid with purchased currency also triggers odds disclosure.
- The simplest compliant position is no paid randomised items at all: sell fixed-content bundles and a pass. If any random reward can be bought directly or indirectly with purchased currency, show the odds in the purchase flow.
- Answer the IARC/App Store rating questionnaires truthfully about simulated gambling; a fictional market is not gambling, a casino mini-game is.
- Player-to-player transfers of valuable items/currency invite real-money trading; rate limits and market-only transfers (no free gifting of high-value assets) reduce both legal ambiguity and cheating.

## 7. Technical choices for a small team: authority model, anti-cheat, backend, bots

### Takeaway
For an economy game the server must own every balance change and validate every purchase and reward; client-trusted or offline-first balances cannot coexist with a shared market or leaderboard. Among backends, Firebase/Supabase are usage-priced general tools where the studio writes the economy logic, Nakama is open source with paid managed hosting, and PlayFab's free path has reportedly narrowed; reliable current prices were not obtained.

### Cited Findings
- Server-authoritative principle: the server performs all crucial checks and refuses to trust client input; validate every purchase and reward grant on the server and compute rewards there. [academic review + practitioner guide] — [Systematic review of technical defences against cheating (arXiv 2512.21377)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21377v1); [DEV: Anti-cheat for small multiplayer games](https://dev.to/aplusgamesstudio/anti-cheat-for-small-multiplayer-games-what-works-4a6g)
- For mobile, layer Google Play Integrity and Apple App Attest plus server-side purchase validation on top of server authority. — [DEV: Anti-cheat for small multiplayer games](https://dev.to/aplusgamesstudio/anti-cheat-for-small-multiplayer-games-what-works-4a6g)
- Even with full server authority, bots can automate legitimate actions; counters are rate limits and behavioural analysis. — [Mirror Networking docs: Cheating](https://mirror-networking.gitbook.io/docs/security/cheating)
- A bot-detection vendor claims 77% of players would quit a multiplayer game if they perceived others cheating; treat as marketing. [vendor claim] — [ClickPatrol](https://clickpatrol.com/mobile-game-bot-detection-in-2025-how-to-detect/)
- Nakama: open-source server with no licence fee; managed Heroic Cloud bills by capacity/custom pricing with no DAU/MAU/CCU limits. [aggregator + competitor vendor blog] — [Toolradar: Nakama pricing](https://toolradar.com/tools/nakama/pricing); [Supercraft: Nakama OSS vs Heroic Cloud](https://crux.supercraft.host/blog/nakama-open-source-vs-managed-backend/)
- PlayFab: 2026 roundups say the free path for non-Xbox indies has narrowed (Development Mode capped at 1,000 lifetime players per title); a comparison site lists paid tiers from $99/month and $600/month. Source is a competing vendor and an aggregator — verify. — [Supercraft: State of game backends Q2 2026](https://crux.supercraft.host/blog/game-backend-2026-q2-state-of-the-stack/); [Toolradar: Nakama vs PlayFab](https://toolradar.com/compare/nakama-vs-playfab)
- Firebase: freemium, usage-based, not game-specific — economy, progression and LiveOps must be built by the studio. — [StartupOG: GameSparks alternatives](https://startupog.com/gamesparks-alternatives)
- Supabase: Postgres backend with realtime and auth; no native multiplayer networking, better for non-realtime games. — [StartupOG: Heroic Labs alternatives](https://startupog.com/heroic-labs-alternatives)
- Backend pricing models differ by usage, tiered and self-hosted; a 2026 comparison (written by a vendor in this market) covers the trade-offs and notes recent service shutdowns as a platform risk. — [Supercraft: pricing models compared](https://gsb.supercraft.host/blog/game-backend-pricing-models-comparison/)
- NPC market makers posting fixed-price orders are used in a live player-economy game to guarantee liquidity for beginners. — [Prosperous Universe beginner guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3545186148)

### Inferences
- A tycoon/trade game is asynchronous: no realtime netcode is needed. The requirement is transactional integrity (atomic buy/sell, no double-spend), which favours a relational database with transactions (Postgres via Supabase or Nakama's Postgres/CockroachDB storage) or carefully written Firestore transactions inside Cloud Functions. All balance mutations should be server functions; clients send intents ("sell 10 of X"), never results.
- Offline/idle earnings can stay cheap: store last-seen timestamp server-side and compute accrued income on the server at next login with caps; never accept a client-reported clock or amount.
- Given the studio already uses AdMob (and likely Firebase), Firebase is the lowest-learning-cost start; its main risks are per-read/write billing with chatty market screens and weaker relational queries for order matching. Cache market prices in a few aggregate documents refreshed on a timer rather than per-trade reads. Supabase/Postgres fits order matching and leaderboards better and has predictable tier pricing; Nakama gives leaderboards, guilds and tournaments out of the box at the cost of running a server.
- Cost should be bounded by the revenue arithmetic in section 5: at an estimated $0.006-0.018 ad ARPDAU, backend spend must stay well under about $0.002 per DAU per day, which rules out designs that poll the server every few seconds.
- Rewarded-ad rewards must be granted server-side (AdMob server-side verification callbacks) or they become the easiest cheat in an ad-monetised economy.
- Bots at launch: NPC traders/companies with server-run simple strategies solve empty markets and leaderboards and are a legitimate design tool (as the market-maker example shows). Label them as NPC firms or keep them in separate brackets; disguising bots as humans risks trust if discovered. No evidence was found on player reaction either way.
- Anti-abuse specific to economies: multi-account feeding through the market (limit trades far from NPC reference price, restrict direct transfers), rate limits per account, device attestation, and anomaly alerts on net-worth growth.

- No source found on Firebase Cloud Functions patterns for validating economy transactions, or on AdMob server-side verification specifics.
- No evidence (studies or postmortems) on bots as substitutes for real multiplayer at launch or on how players perceive them.
- No postmortem from a small studio running a server-authoritative mobile economy game was found.
